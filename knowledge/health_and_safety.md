# Health and Safety

## Routine care

Pets do best with a steady daily routine: regular meals, exercise, and rest at
similar times each day. Consistency lowers stress and makes it easier to notice when
something is wrong, because a change in appetite, energy, or bathroom habits stands
out against a normal baseline.

## Medication

If a pet is prescribed medication, it should be given at the times and doses the vet
specified, and the full course finished even if the pet seems better. Many meds are
given with food. Because a missed dose can matter, medication is a high priority task
that should be scheduled first, not squeezed in at the end of the day.

## Warning signs to watch for

Some symptoms mean a pet should see a vet soon: not eating for more than a day,
repeated vomiting or diarrhea, limping, or unusual lethargy. Tracking these in a
daily routine helps an owner catch problems early.

## When it is an emergency

Certain situations are emergencies and need a vet or emergency clinic right away,
not a daily plan. These include trouble breathing, choking, a seizure, heavy or
non-stop bleeding, a suspected poisoning, a swollen hard belly, or collapse. This
care guide is for routine planning only and cannot diagnose or treat a sick animal.
When in doubt about a pet's health, contact a veterinarian.
